# CP-001 — Rwanda to College to Aspen Offer

**Status:** FROZEN  
**Type:** chronological excavation checkpoint  
**Boundary:** childhood/education in Rwanda through acceptance of first Aspen Technology offer

## Purpose
This checkpoint freezes the first bounded section of the career excavation. It is a navigational and interpretive layer, not a replacement for primary testimony.

## Chronological map

### Rwanda: early formation
- Grew up and attended primary/secondary school in Rwanda.
- Attended a Catholic/minor-seminary school with a strong STEM concentration.
- Excelled particularly in biology; medicine/surgery appeared to be the expected path.
- Learned as a teenager about scholarship opportunities for Rwandan students to study in the United States and focused on grades and English.
- Early encounters with computers and the internet were limited but memorable, including using a cybercafe to exchange email and photographs with relatives abroad.

### United States and CBU
- Received scholarship support to attend California Baptist University.
- Entered Electrical & Computer Engineering largely through placement rather than a long-standing plan.
- Became fascinated by understanding how software, email, websites, and technical systems actually worked.
- Early C++ programming was difficult partly because ordinary English words such as “class” and “object” carried unfamiliar technical meanings.

### Church mobile-app project
- Worked with classmate Ryan on a church-related mobile application project.
- Technical implementation contribution was initially limited, but the project exposed a recurring strength: investigating what stakeholders possessed, wanted, and meant, and identifying mismatches between user needs, pastor expectations, and technical/data reality.
- Began forming a mental model of software work as translation between human intent and technical structure.

### Campus work and KIST internship
- Worked campus jobs including food-service/dishwashing and Conferences & Events work.
- Returned to Rwanda for a scholarship-required internship at Kigali Institute of Science and Technology (KIST).
- Encountered Drupal, CMSs, websites, servers, and web terminology without prior direct experience.
- Learned to identify unknown terminology, research independently, restate technical problems in plain language, and confirm shared understanding with stakeholders.
- Recognized that “software” spans many domains and that formal coursework alone would not automatically produce the career he wanted.

### Software-career wake-up
- Saw classmate Ryan gain substantial professional experience and income through a software internship.
- Researched software-engineering salaries and job descriptions.
- Discovered gaps between his ECE curriculum and common software-engineering interview/job requirements such as algorithms, data structures, and coding-interview preparation.

### Mentor conversation: “professional dishwasher”
- Sought out a working web developer through church/network connections and asked what he should do to become a software engineer.
- The mentor challenged the mismatch between Nelly's stated ambition and how he was spending his discretionary work time.
- Central lesson: employers need evidence of what distinguishes you; unassigned time can demonstrate what you are actually becoming.
- Nelly changed his allocation of time toward software-relevant experience.

### Digital library automation
- Proactively found an on-campus opportunity with the Digital Librarian.
- Investigated labor-intensive spreadsheet work used to evaluate digital-library packages, overlap, relevance, holdings, and cost.
- Proposed automating repetitive comparison work and obtained a paid student role.
- Taught himself Perl by transferring concepts learned in C++.
- Worked with messy/incomplete data, separated usable from incomplete records, automated comparisons, and iterated directly with the stakeholder.
- Gained practical experience in end-to-end problem solving, stakeholder communication, decomposition, interfaces, reuse, performance reasoning, and learning new technologies independently.

### Dr. Ricard / glaucoma research
- Asked Differential Equations professor Dr. Ricard what research he would pursue if he had time/resources and volunteered summer labor.
- Learned an unfamiliar ophthalmology/fluid-mechanics domain by reading papers and decoding its vocabulary.
- Developed a cross-domain model connecting fluid/hydraulic behavior with electrical-circuit concepts such as pressure/voltage, flow/current, and resistance.
- Participated in physical/modeling experimentation and C++-based analysis.
- Produced a research paper and presented work at an ophthalmology/vision-research conference in Seattle.
- Publication authorship, conference naming/firsts, experimental apparatus details, and medical interpretations remain candidates for artifact-based verification before external publication.

### Aspen offer
- Applied broadly for entry-level software roles.
- Received an offer from Aspen Technology in New Hampshire at approximately $60,000.
- Accepted the offer, marking the transition into his first corporate software-engineering role.
